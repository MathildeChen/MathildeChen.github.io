Où poussent les bananiers ?  🍌 


**Auteurs :** Jérémy LAVARENNE, Pierre BONNET, Marie DAGUIER, Lucie MICHEL, Mathilde CHEN

Pour qu'un bioagresseur s'établisse sur un territoire, il fuat bien qu'il se mette quelque chose sous la dent ... pour la fusariose race tropicale 4 (FocTR4), il s'agit de bananiers. Les bananes, dessert ou à cuire, sont cultivées dans des exploitations agricoles.

**Principaux éléments abordés dans la communication :** La communication a pour but de présenter une approche exploratoire pour cartographier la distribution de la présence des plantes du genre Musa dans les territoires d’Outre-Mer pour informer la surveillance de la Fusariose race tropicale 4 (FocTR4) du bananier. Après avoir fourni des éléments de contexte sur la maladie, la méthode est présentée : elle s’appuie sur la collecte d’images « street-view », leur analyse avec le modèle d’identification Pl@ntNet, et l’utilisation des occurrences issues de ces identifications dans des modélisations de niche. Des premiers résultats sont présentés, puis les limites et perspectives de ce travail. 


➡️ **Abstract:** Fusarium wilt tropical race 4 (FocTR4) is one of the most destructive diseases threatening global banana production. The disease affects a wide range of cultivars, including those of the ‘Cavendish’ subgroup, provoking major losses in cultivated banana plantations. Musa plants could also contribute to the spread and survival of the disease outside of commercial plantations, but gaps in the information on the distribution of these plants hamper the inclusion of such information in epidemiological models. The objective of this study was to explore the potential to model the distribution of Musa species in Martinique from automated identification of opportunistic images. Georeferenced onboard vehicle camera images were drawn from the open-source Mapillary® dataset using a 0.01 x 0.01° sliding window to retrieve available images along roads or other traversable paths of the island. The Mapillary API (https://www.mapillary.com/?locale=fr_FR) was queried on 8 January 2026, while image capture dates varied. The resulting 182,493 images were analyzed by the Pl@ntNet API (https://my.plantnet.org/doc/api/openapi) for automatic species recognition based on the wide-use identification model (i.e., “k-world-flora project”). Several identification confidence thresholds were compared. Within each 100 m grid cell, the detection with the highest Pl@ntNet score was retained. The number of potential occurrences of Musa sp. in Martinique ranged from 750 at an identification confidence threshold of 0.1 to 0 at a threshold of 0.9. To spatially extrapolate candidate occurrence locations across Martinique, maxent species distribution models were fitted to these candidate occurrences using the 64-dimensional features generated by AlphaEarth Foundations, which capture temporal trajectories of surface conditions, aggregated from 10-m to 100-m pixels. Background points were selected using a bias layer based on Mapillary coverage. This exploratory work combining opportunistic observations and AI tools could contribute to FocTR4 monitoring by identifying areas with a higher predicted occurrence of Musa. Further steps include manual validation of Pl@ntNet detections, external evaluation of the developed niche distribution model’s predictions against Musa records in plants observations databases (e.g., GBIF and iNaturalist), the combination with other risk factors in order to refine the risk assessment and the application to other territories where the disease is present (e.g., Mayotte island in the Indian Ocean) in order to estimate the added value of the derived prioritization map to non-targeted surveillance.
